# Rivals plan: the rival engine, the Lamplighter and recovered pages

> Design: [rivals.md](../design/rivals.md), [rune-design-final.md](../design/rune-design-final.md) §3, §7, §10, [footnotes.md](../design/footnotes.md) §1 "Rival notes", [city-and-districts.md](../design/city-and-districts.md) §5b. Roadmap: **BL-30**, **BL-118**. Prefix **RV**. Brief: [build-program.md](build-program.md).
> State: **Drafted (not approved)**.
> Scope: foes that cast real lattices under the shared rune rules, with locked aims, honest telegraphs, the starved-chain fallback and deterministic routines; the canal Lamplighter with the long wick as a Lab prototype behind a **player playtest gate**; the authoring API every district uses for its rival; recovered annotated pages (banking, notebook page, Bench trial copy, witness and credit rules, the rival-note hook, the "pages held" count).

## 1. Scope and settled rules

**Delivers**, in three phases:

1. **Rival engine and the Lamplighter Lab prototype.** A rival is an enemy whose actions are spells compiled from a lattice with the same compiler and resolver as the household (R1–R9, J1–J5). Its telegraph shows path, landing, stored effects due to open and the conditional fallback (rivals §1). Its native aim is locked (rivals §1). A fully starved action may be followed by a 1-damage strike at a pre-shown adjacent tile (rivals §1). Its routine is a deterministic priority list (rivals §2). The long wick is an **object lock** on one named jar (rivals §2). The Lamplighter is played first alone in the Lab, then with canal foes. Then the phase ends at **gate RV-G1**.
2. **Rival authoring API.** A district plan defines a rival as data: spells, routine, signature trick, numbers and page. The trick plugs into named hook points, so the district never edits engine internals. This phase is tested against the five known tricks (§3 P10); those rivals are not built here.
3. **Recovered pages (BL-118).** Pages are banked on victory, shown as an annotated notebook page and tried as a Bench trial copy with isolation. This phase also covers the witness and credit rules, the rival-note slot hook and `pagesHeld`.

**Settled by design (cited, not repeated):** the player's choices in rivals §0 (same runes plus one trick, breaking chains as the best moment, routine with responses, avoidable fallback, intact annotated page, the long wick, trial copy). The Lamplighter's placement in canal room 4 with the wardens' pass as a separate conditional offer (city §5b). The proposed kit `Bolt → Jar → Oil → Spark` / `Bolt → Oil → Shove` and the starting numbers (rivals §2). The page rules (rivals §3). The rival-note rule (footnotes §1).

**Leaves out:** the other five rivals' lattices, tricks and pages (IQ, BH, BR, BF, CG), placing the Lamplighter in room 4 and the key offer (CN), footnote level mechanics and slot rendering (FN), hearing kinds (DK), scripted stand-in hearts (DK), the arrival pipeline and event contracts (FD).

Second, walk with `enemyWalk`. Third, re-plan the action from the arrived tile without moving. A plan whose outcome has no effect is never telegraphed. Ties go to the shorter move, then the destination in reading order, then the aim in `DIRS` order | Honest scoring with the real rules, and no search over unseen party actions. ## 3. Current implementation and risks

- `ai.ts` `act` dispatches by `EnemyDef.ai`. Canal AIs go to `foes.ts` `foeAct`. Planning is BFS, rng-free and in unit order, and round 1 has `move: false`. A new `'rival'` branch fits here. `nearestMember` ranks ties by the fixed `MEMBER_IDS`, which HC replaces.
- `resolveSpell(ctx, caster: Unit, …)` and `aimTargets` already accept an enemy caster. `giftedIn` is false and `habit` null for enemies. `trace` is emitted only for members, so rival casts already stay out of proof. `runContinuation` attributes stored flow by `Origin`. `spellOf`, `legalAims`, `castableSpells` and `aimPreview` are member-only and need rival twins.
- `jars.ts`: owners are plain `UnitId`, `jarLimit` is 1 for an enemy, and step-jars open via the `entered` hook, so a herd into the jar works once FD's arrival pipeline routes pushes there. Jars have no stable id (only `seq`). The wick lock needs FD's jar identity.
- `turn.ts` `resolveIntent` handles `attack` and the K-17 specials. A stunned foe skips the action, and that path must not run the fallback. `Intent` is switched on in `view.ts`, `overlay.ts` (`intentPreview` handles attacks only, `marksOf` handles summon and flare), `describe.ts` `intentPhrase` and the playback view. **Risk:** every switch site has to learn the new kinds.
- The player preview (`battleInput.ts` `Preview`: `shown`, `blanks`, `hurt`, `masked`) is the masking model to reuse. The overlay already draws intents from `preview.result.state`.
- `run.ts` `learnFromFight` and `finishEncounter` are shared with DK and the district plans. RV adds one call there (banking) and keeps the rest in `src/engine/rivals/pages.ts`.
- The Bench (`bench.ts` `Draft`, `sceneBattle`, `trialLattice`) is built around inventions, so page trials need a second mode. `sceneItems` offers every `foesMet`, which P9 filters.
- `Profile` (types.ts) has no pages, and the save is at version 8 with HC's breaking reset pending. **Risk:** land `pages` after or with HC's reset to avoid a migration.
- **Risk: performance.** A Lamplighter plan is at most 5 destinations × (2 spells × 4 aims + wick), about 45 simulated casts, under the 500-event cap per cast. The preview is memoised per state.

## 4. Contracts

**Provides**

- `RivalDef` (data): `id`, enemy numbers (hp, move, token, name, elite), `spells: RivalSpellDef[]` (`id`, name, lattice notation, charges), `routine: GoalStep[]`, `trick: TrickId | null`, `fallback: {damage}`, `dryStrike: AttackId`, `page: RivalPageId | null`. A district registers its defs through DK's module contract (RV-W0).
- `TrickDef` (engine, per district module) with the hook points of P10: `plan`, `resolve`, `mark`, `beforeHarm`, `resists`, `roundEnd`, `onDeath`, and a `draw` descriptor (tiles, units, objects, sequence `n/m`, countdown, label) that the generic UI renders. Also trick-owned objects with ids and lock semantics.
- Goal vocabulary: `hitParty`, `herdInto(tiles | objectKind)`, `openOwnObject`, `placeObjectNear`, `approach`, `holdDistance`. Plus `registerGoal` for district goals. `Outcome` metrics from the simulation evaluator.
- `rivalPreview(state, id)`, `isEffect(event)`, `isStarved(events)`.
- `RivalPageDef`: `id`, rival, the recovered spell's lattice, annotation, story, trial scene, `note: {rune, line, variant}`. Plus `holdsPage(p, id)`, `pagesHeld(p): RivalPageId[]`, `rivalNoteVisible(p, variant)`.
- Event types for the log: `rivalPlans`, `fallbackStrike`, `orderFails` (a lock lost), `pageRecovered`.

**Consumes**

- **FD:** the arrival pipeline (a shove or slide onto a step-jar opens it, and the immunity and relocation hooks sit on it), authoritative event contracts (for `isEffect`), stable jar and object ids, the 500-event cap. The task IDs are those of FD's wave 0 and its arrival, event-contract and jar-identity tasks.
- **DK:** the district module contract (how a district registers rivals, tricks and goals), the round-end board-rule hook, the board-piece registry (keystone, bells, stamp), the out-of-the-room state (erased units), the hearing-kind extension (it reads `pagesHeld`). The scripted stand-in hearts are DK's and carry no page.
- **HC:** the variable party and the roster order used for tie-breaking (planning reads the battle's member order, not `MEMBER_IDS`), and the save reset that `Profile.pages` rides on.
- **FN:** the variant schema and the rival-note slot rendering. RV supplies the Lamplighter's note as a variant entry in FN's format. **Cross-plan gap:** the note sits on Jar, a Timing rune outside FN's Seam pass. Some plan (CN or FN's canal batch) must write Jar's level slots, otherwise the note stays hidden.
- **CN:** places the Lamplighter (`RivalDef` `lamplighter`, token `L`) in re-laid room 4 and keeps the wardens' pass as a normal conditional offer.
- **AH:** Pull, Hop and Swap, used as counters in scenario tests once they are built (not required by the gate).

## 7. Playtest gate RV-G1: what the player judges (Lab, phone)

1. **Style:** does he read as a lamplighter (lay, herd, wick) within two or three rounds, and do his plans stay sensible when the lantern is gone or out of reach?
2. **Locked aim:** does a displaced Bolt caster firing a parallel line feel fair and predictable? Is it obvious he does not follow a moved apprentice?
3. **Breaking his chain:** does starving a spell feel like the best moment? Is the pre-shown fallback strike readable, avoidable and not punishing? Is 1 damage right?
4. **The long wick:** is the "opens this jar next" telegraph clear? Does the drift case read? Is it a fair threat, or too strong when combined with herding?
5. **Self-trap:** can you reliably shove him into his own lantern, and does that feel earned?
6. **Solvable with Seam and canal tools** (no Erase or Draw needed), alone and with canal foes. Is he an elite or a mini-boss in room 4?
7. **Numbers:** 6 HP, move 1, five charges, one jar: too long or too short a fight?
8. **Phone readability:** path, landing, blank unknown lines, harm warning, fallback ring and wick line on a crowded canal board; the rival sheet.
