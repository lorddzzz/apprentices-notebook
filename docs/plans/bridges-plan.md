# The bridges: implementation plan (BR)

Design: [district-bridges.md](../design/district-bridges.md) (all), [city-and-districts.md](../design/city-and-districts.md) §3.2 and §5b, [rune-design-final.md](../design/rune-design-final.md) §4.4, §5.2, §6.2, §6.3, §11, [party.md](../design/party.md) §2 (the defector warden), [rivals.md](../design/rivals.md). Roadmap: **BL-115**. Prefix **BR**. Brief: [build-program.md](build-program.md).
**State: Drafted (not approved).**
**Scope:** the arches in the fog as a district module on DK's kit: piers and falls, span choice, four rooms and the heart, drop edges, the wardens' staff, the Chief Warden on the keystone (stand-in first, rival after RV), the Wall hearing, the key gate, mason's-ledger lore, the warden's landmark room, a lower-Seam intrusion, the footnote batch, the Bind *pushed* page, Bench parity and calibration.

## 1. Scope and settled rules

**Delivers**, all as files of the bridges module (DK P1: `src/data/districts/bridges.ts`, `src/engine/districts/bridges.ts`, `src/ui/districts/bridges/`):

- **The collapse rule.** A span stands only while connected to a pier; cut-off sections are cracked at once and fall at dusk with everything on them (design §0, §1 "Collapse timing").
- **Falls.** Members leave the room until it ends and are hauled back at 1 HP on a win; a collapse that takes the whole party is a wipe. Minions are gone, elites take 4 and lose their next action (design §0, DK-6).
- **Trip shape.** Choose your span after each room (sound long or crumbling short), return free after any room, a Crossroads that carries a mason's-ledger line.
- **Content.** Rooms, foes (Warden, Gargoyle, Mason), the heart, loot tables, the recruit's landmark room, the key gate, the Wall hearing, the *pushed* Bind page, lore texts.
- **Also:** the lower-Seam intrusion, the Structure footnote batch, Bench parity, bot calibration.

**Settled by design (cited, not re-decided):** the arches-in-the-fog theme, the defector's place in the district, drop edges for bridge rooms only (the Seam and canals keep their walls and water; `EdgeRule` exists), the canals' drop unchanged (city §5b), the key's rules (loot offer, lost on a wipe, banked at home, one per trip, only once the bridges are heard), the Chief Warden's right of way (can't be pushed, held or felled while the keystone stands) and that the bridge falls after the win with no party penalty.

**Leaves out:** the household, recruit and action systems (HC, RC, AH), the offer card for the key and its placement in the canals (CN), rival engine and tricks API (RV), the lower-Seam floor framework (SM), art beyond what the board needs, audio.

## 2. Proposals

| # | Proposal | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Trip length reading.** "Five rooms and the heart, so four span choices before the heart" only adds up if the heart is the fifth room, as in the canals: **rooms 1–4 plus the heart = 5 encounters, 4 span choices** (after rooms 1, 2, 3, 4; the last picks the approach to the great arch) | The two sentences otherwise disagree (six rooms would mean five choices) |
| P2 | **The support model.** Three pieces from DK's registry: `{span}` (floor that can fall), `{pier}` (a standable footing: never worn, never falls) and `{keystone}` (a pier with HP). The network is the span tiles and the piers, 4-connected. A span tile is **supported** when its component contains a pier. **Cracked** = every span tile that is unsupported or broken. The cracked set is *derived* from the board whenever it is asked for, never stored, so previews and replays cannot disagree. Bridge rooms get `{span}` on every standable non-pier floor tile from `roomSetup` board edits, so layouts stay ordinary ASCII with `_` for fog and `{pier}` where piers stand; the intrusion and the Lab write `{span}` explicitly | The design says "works like clouds and brass: a network you can cut"; DK's `componentsOf` is exactly the check. Authoring stays readable |
| P3 | **How a link breaks.** The design lists "a crush, a burst, a pushed pier-stone" but no object that breaks. This plan makes the **span tiles themselves** the breakable links: each has *stone* (2 on sound spans, 1 on crumbling, room data). A **crush** wears 1 off the tile the crushed unit rests on; a **burst** (storm, pool, kettle, wall ring, detonation, conduct; **not fire**) wears 1 off each tile it covers. At 0 the tile is **broken**: removed from the network at once and part of the cracked set. Piers do not wear (the keystone does, P7). The "pushed pier-stone" is dropped: piers never move | Gives Spark and Shove a role, makes sound spans solid and crumbling ones fragile (1 stone, few piers) with one rule, and keeps the cut a deliberate act |
| P4 | **Dusk collapse.** One rule `bridges.collapse` in DK's `late` slot (after snuffers, so a storm that wears stone at dusk falls the same dusk). Cracks are drawn as soon as they exist; **foes do not move between a cut and the fall** because their planning follows dusk (the design's "foes don't dodge"). The cutter must walk off or sacrifice. A cut made in the foes' resolve phase falls the same dusk; the foes' telegraphs are the warning | Simple, matches the design default, no per-tile timers |
| P5 | **Fall outcomes.** Members: DK-6 `removeFromRoom(…, 'fell')`. Minion: `dropped` event, gone, no death-burst. Elite: `dropped`, 4 damage, stunned, then set on the **nearest standing tile** (reading-order ties) through FD's arrival pipeline (cause `landed`). Fliers (Gargoyle) ignore floors. States, jars and marks on the tile vanish with it (existing fade/crumble events). The fallen tile becomes a drop tile (`terrain.drop`), so Brink, pushes and Mortar already treat it as an edge | Reuses the built drop wording; DK-6 does not say where a surviving elite stands, so BR does (request R1) |
| P6 | **The Long Drop counts collapses.** A foe that falls with its span emits `dropped` credited to the origin of the cut (stored per broken tile), so The Long Drop names, proof and ledger credit work. **Redefines:** the idiom's rule text "pushed off an edge or into a canal" gains "or dropped with a cut span" (`data/idioms.ts`, `idioms.ts`) | The design calls the collapse "a plan, not an accident" and the Long Drop its idiom |
| P7 | **The keystone.** HP 6 (*tune by playing*). A crush that rests on or hits the keystone tile, or a burst covering it, wears 1. At 0 it **shatters**: the tile becomes fog, the Chief Warden loses right of way for good, falls like an elite (P5), and sections held only by the keystone crack. He keeps his ordinary HP (10, *tune*), so plain damage also wins; the keystone is the route that makes him pushable, holdable and droppable. When he dies the remaining span falls **after the win** (a scripted epilogue event; nobody is out of the room) | Matches "crush and burst it to bring him down" and "the bridge falls after the win" without a second boss bar |
| P8 | **The wardens' staff** (*numbers tune by playing*; tokens are placeholders, see request R5). **Warden** `k`: HP 4, move 2, elite; telegraphs a **chalk circle** on a unit within 3 in a straight line; at resolve the unit still there is held until after the party's next turn (the Hold rule, so it also blocks walking off a cracked tile). **Gargoyle** `y`: HP 3, move 3, flies, not an elite; telegraphs a **carry**: a named apprentice and the tile 2 steps toward the nearest fog it will set them on (preferring a cracked tile); resolved through FD's `carry` arrival; a held unit cannot be carried, so Hold protects. **Mason** `a`: HP 3, move 2; telegraphs a **mend**: rebuild an adjacent fog tile that was original span, preferring a link that rescues a cracked section, else lay a pier on a span tile far from any pier. A tile with a unit on it cannot be mended. Damage is minimal; the threat is the floor. Crumbling rooms have fewer piers and fewer Masons | Each foe acts on piers and drops as designed, each telegraph names its target |
| P9 | **Foes ignore cracks** when walking (v1). A foe stands where the AI puts it and falls with its section if the party cut it | Keeps the AI change out of scope; revisit only if collapses feel too free |
| P10 | **Layouts.** Room 1: 3 sound layouts (nothing to choose before it). Rooms 2–4: **2 sound + 2 crumbling** each. Heart: **2** (sound approach: more guards and piers; crumbling approach: fewer guards and a thinner arch). That is 17 boards; a third per kind is added only if tuning wants variety. Sound boards are 6×9–7×9, crumbling 5×6–6×7, every board has ≥4 `P` start points (HC `checkRoom`) and `edge: 'drop'` | The span choice already doubles variety. Seventeen is authorable and the design's "three per depth" stays open |
| P11 | **Crossroads and tables.** The module replaces Push on with two labelled choices (sound long / crumbling short) plus Return. Sound: more foes, table `bridgeSound`. Crumbling: fewer foes, table `bridgeCrumbling` (no ink, extra district runes, solvents, vellum). Return is `ret: { kind: 'any' }` including after the heart | "Better loot" made concrete; the heart stays a return point (DK fuzz check) |

## 3. Current implementation and risks

- `data/districts.ts` holds `bridges` as a `drawn()` stub: material `brass`, `edge` wall, placeholder hearing (`named idiom:theWall`, trips ≥ 5). DK replaces it with a module file.
- `push.ts`: `stopAt` already returns a `drop` for `terrain.drop` tiles and for the board edge when `s.edge === 'drop'` (fliers excepted); `fall` implements minion gone, elite 4 and stun; `crush` wears plates via `wearPlate`. Brink (`runes/flow.ts` `beside`) already counts drop tiles and drop edges. `notify('pushed')` exists, so Anchor works; only learning the event is missing.
- `turn.ts` `hazards` ends with `snuffStep`; DK-3 adds the slots.
- `ENEMIES` has no tokens free for the staff: `w`, `g`, `l`, `h`, `i`, `o`, `x` are terrain or states in `notation.ts`. Lowercase `k`, `y`, `a` and `K` are unclaimed today.
- The bot (`tests/sim/bot.ts`) values harm only and knows nothing about cracks; it would fall into every collapse.
- **Risks:** (1) the engine's `onStruck` for tiles (DK-4) must report both the crushed unit's resting tile and the obstacle tile, or wear cannot be built (R1); (2) the elite's re-landing after a fall is a new arrival cause; (3) a 5×6 crumbling board leaves very little room to walk off cracks (phone readability and fairness for 5–6 HP bodies); (4) hot files: `push.ts` and `idioms.ts` are shared with FN's batches; keep BR edits to one-line branches; (5) cracks and fog art at 375×812 are an open design question, answered in BR-14.
