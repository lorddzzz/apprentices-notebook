# The Party: Apprentices

> **Status: Designed** (scoped 2026-10-18; household systems 2026-10-22 in §0b; **basic actions, habit rules, door arrivals and all nine apprentices 2026-10-23 in §0c, §1 and §2**). Not built beyond the current two. Roadmap: BL-43.
> Summary and decision log: [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) §6.1, §6.2 and §11. **Supersedes** the 2026-10-12 decisions "members differ by material affinity and lattice shape" and "each lattice is a small grid shaped by its member". Related: [city-and-districts.md](city-and-districts.md) (guild schools), [rivals.md](rivals.md), [current rune rules](rune-design-final.md) §9.

## Current implementation boundary (2026-10-21)

Two apprentices remain playable. Both move 3 and share basic Shove. The Draughtsman has 6 HP, trained Timing, gifted Contracts and the two-jars habit; the Stoker has 8 HP, trained/gifted Seam and Fork-ahead. Training discounts school runes by 1 ink (minimum 1); gifts improve the rune's declared range, push or duration. Ink is currently per apprentice, with household reserve upgrades increasing both allowances.

**Growing pages are built (BL-103/88):** both apprentices use paper that grows in all directions, phone pan/Fit and the same freedom in Glyph revision. Fixed geometry remains available for authored scenario layouts. Details: [growing-pages.md](growing-pages.md). Recruitment, selection of three, larger rooms and personal basic actions remain BL-43. No new recruit needs an aspect pair or a material affinity.

## 0. Decided in the scoping round (2026-10-18)

| Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where apprentices come from | **Both: found in the districts and at the door.** Some are recruited in a guild district (a runaway, a clerk) and arrive trained in that guild's school; others turn up at the attic over time (rumours, letters) | The city map is also a recruitment map, and the attic gets its own slow trickle |
| Who goes on a trip | **Pick 3 of many.** The household grows, and the party is 3 | Bigger combos. The trio is a build decision, like wiring |
| What makes one different | **School lean** (one or two schools they're trained in, cheaper or stronger there; replaces material affinity), **a habit** (one small passive rule that bends how their lattice reads), **a body on the board** (HP, move and a personal basic action instead of everyone's Shove: pull, swap, hop…) | Identity by how they play, not by what they're allowed to wire |
| Lattice shape | **Removed.** No fixed member shape. A lattice is a page that **widens as needed**, like the Fair Copy and revision canvas | Fixed shapes limited exploration |
| What limits a lattice | **Ink only** | One budget, as now. Long chains and wide forks compete for the same ink |
| The board | **A little bigger, about 7×9** if it still fits portrait well; rooms are re-laid (*tune by playing*) | Three apprentices plus foes crowd 6×8 |
| How their magic meets | **One feeds the other**: one apprentice's spell leaves a state or trips a trigger that the next one's chain cashes in that same turn. **Casting order is the combo** | Triggers that watch the board make this natural |
| Growth | **The notebook carries it.** Runes, Glyphs, footnotes and ink grow; apprentices are who you bring and how they lean. A new recruit is useful at once | Keeps the build in the runes |


## 0b. Decided in the household round (2026-10-22)

This round fixed the **systems**: how many, how recruited, how picked, how deployed. It deliberately designed **no specific apprentice**; the roster came a day later in §0c and §2. Where this table differs from §0, this table wins.

| Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Household size | **About 8–9 in all**: the current two, about one found per middle district (four), and two or three door arrivals (*Proposal*: the Cartographers add none; tune by playing) | Every pick of three is a real choice, and each apprentice can still be authored with care |
| Lattice pages | **Each apprentice keeps their own page, forever.** Swapping the trio never means rewiring; a recruit arrives with a working starter page. A "copy page from…" helper is a later polish idea, not decided | Wiring is part of who they are; keeps their lean and habit tied to their lattice |
| Recruiting in a district | **A loot offer you can pick** ("someone joins you", like the quiet doorway). Taking it costs that pick. Each district's recruit is a fixed, authored apprentice at a landmark room; **if declined, they are offered again on later trips** until taken (*Proposal*: one per district) | Fits the existing offer system and keeps recruiting a real choice against runes and ink, without losing anyone for good |
| Recruiting at the door | **Free arrivals at home, triggered by notebook milestones** (first idiom witnessed, a Glyph reaching a tier, a proven invention…). Someone has heard of your work. Exact milestones are decided per apprentice | The notebook carries growth; arrivals recognise what the player built |
| What a recruit brings | **Only a starter page**, built from runes the household already owns, plus body, lean, action and habit. No new rune page, no signature Glyph | Districts keep control of when each school's runes arrive; signature pages overlap BL-118 |
| When the trio first exists | **The third apprentice comes from the canals** (a district recruit offer). The Seam is a duo until then; the trio can return to any room | The first district-found recruit teaches recruiting by itself |
| Where the third waits | **The canals' second landing** (after room 3), as the first district-found recruit offer ([city-and-districts.md](city-and-districts.md) §5b). *(Decided 2026-10-22, canals revisit)* | The safe middle stop |
| Choosing the trio | **At home, in one household shelf in the Study**: portrait cards with the three trip slots on top and the rest on a bench. Tap a card to open that apprentice's page; tap or drag to fill a slot. **Fixed for the whole trip** (no doorway swap, no per-fight swap) | Wiring and picking in one place; swapping mid-trip would undercut the rare doorway and the push-or-return tension |
| Fewer than three | **Allowed: 1–3 apprentices.** A pure challenge with **no scaling and no bonus**: rooms and loot stay the same; fewer casts and less total ink. *Tune by playing* if it proves a trap or an exploit | No special rules to build or exploit; you choose it for a story, a combo or a test |
| Ink | **Uniform for everyone**, raised by the household's reserve upgrades; every recruit gets the current reserve (*Proposal*). Ink is not part of identity | Identity is body, lean, action and habit; a recruit is comparable and useful at once |
| Kit parts | Every apprentice has a **body** (HP, move), **exactly one personal basic action** that replaces Shove, a **school lean** and **one unique habit**. **Revised 2026-10-22 (same day):** the action is *not* picked from a menu; it is chosen to **fit that apprentice's narrative**. Actions may be **shared by several apprentices**, so we never invent a needlessly complex or odd action just for variety | Actions stay cheap and readable, and they come from who the apprentice is; habits carry personality |
| Falling in battle | **Unchanged from the build:** a downed apprentice returns at 1 HP after a won fight; the trip is wiped when all are down (*Proposal*, not asked) | Keeps attrition as it is |
| Board size | **Per room, for narrative, not for party size** (this replaces §0's "about 7×9" and DESIGN §6.4's fixed 6×8). Rooms are re-laid with start points instead of the named Draughtsman and Stoker | The room shape serves the place and the foes |
| Starting spots | Each board defines **more than three start points** (at least four); the party's spots are **drawn at random, each time, from the trip seed** through the engine's seeded rng, so replays and tests stay deterministic. A smaller party uses a random subset. Foe telegraphs show immediately, as now | Authored safety plus variety; keeps the engine deterministic. Authors must keep every start point fair for a frail body (*tune by playing*) |

## 0c. Decided in the roster round (2026-10-23)

This round settled everything §0b left open: the four basic actions, the habit rules, door milestones, and **all nine apprentices**. Names are **placeholders** (BL-40); the player picked each sketch from three, so the unchosen alternatives are not kept (they are in the commit that adds this section, per the design README's lifecycle).

| Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Basic actions | **Four: Shove, Pull, Hop, Swap.** Rules in §1. Each is shared by two or three apprentices | Each opens a different board trick; none heals or damages by itself, so the lattice stays the source of power |
| Habit rule | **A habit changes one thing: how her lattice reads, or one rule about how the board treats her body.** Guidelines in §1 | The player chose room for body habits over lattice-only; the guidelines keep both previewable and testable |
| Door arrivals | **Two**, each answering a notebook milestone tied to a school you hold and carrying that school's lean. A: after the first witnessed idiom, a Seam-school generalist. B: late, a Board-school exile. Details in §2 | "Someone has read your work"; never idle, never a tease for a school you cannot yet use |
| Household total | **Nine**: the pair, the canals' runaway, four middle-district recruits, two door arrivals. The Cartographers' hall adds none. A tenth after the ending is **parked** | Matches §0b's 8–9 |
| Copy page from… | **Not now.** Each recruit's starter page is enough; revisit if a tenth arrives | Nothing in a nine-person roster needs it |
| Saves | **A household roster is a breaking save change: incompatible saves are cleared, not migrated** (the player's call). A fresh save starts with the pair and an empty bench | No migration code; the project is still pre-release |

## 1. Rules

### Basic actions

All four use the **uncharged cast slot** (move + one cast per turn is unchanged), need no ink and are always available. Each shows its landing in the preview like a cast. Numbers and edge cases are *Proposals*, *tune by playing*.

| Action | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Shove** | Built: push an adjacent unit 1 tile along the heading |
| **Pull** | Hooks a unit **2 tiles away in a straight line**: it moves 1 tile toward the caster and stops adjacent. It slides on oil like any push, is blocked (no crush) by anything in the way, and a pulled unit that reaches fire, a jar or a plate triggers it as an arrival (BL-108). A foe adjacent already is not pulled |
| **Hop** | Leap to a **free tile exactly 2 away in a straight line**, over a unit, jar or cut. Cannot cross a wall; lands as an arrival on that tile. Spends the action, not the move |
| **Swap** | Trade places with an **adjacent unit**, ally or foe (not anchored or flying foes). Both land as arrivals, so swapping a foe onto a wet tile or an ally out of a telegraph is the point, and swapping a frail body next to a foe is the risk |

### Habits

- A **lattice habit** bends one headline of one rune or role, **only on that apprentice's own page and casts**. It is dormant (shown "needs Tally" on her card, not hidden) when the rune is not yet owned.
- A **body habit** is one rule about how the board treats her body, never a stat. It never covers the other two apprentices.
- A habit **never adds damage, ink or a free cast**, never scales with party size, has a one-sentence name on the card, and is **shown in the preview** when it changes a landing. Every habit ships with a scenario test (see the `scenario` skill) and its fine print is witnessed like a rune's.
- A foe-side equivalent (a rival's trick) can use the same hook, so habits double as the rivals' "one trick" ([rivals.md](rivals.md)).

### The household shelf and trio

- **One shelf in the Study:** portrait cards (token letter, colour *and* shape for colour-blind readability, HP and move, action icon, lean chips, the habit line, a dormant flag), three trip slots at the top, the rest on a scrolling bench. Tap a card to open that apprentice's page; tap or drag to fill a slot. Locked during a trip. Joining apprentices land on the bench; they never auto-fill a slot.
- **Tokens (one letter each, unique):** D S R N B W T C E (see roster).
- **Trip ledger and trip page** credit lines to the apprentice's token; the **Lab** can field any apprentices.
- **Start points and boards:** per-room sizes stay between about 5×6 and 7×9 (*tune by playing*); every room defines at least four start points, and a fair start for the frailest body in the household (5–6 HP) is the authoring check.

### Existing pair

Unchanged: the Draughtsman and the Stoker keep their bodies, leans, habits and Shove. They are the baseline: HP 6–9, move 2–3, and nobody is better than the pair at everything.

Action tally: Shove 3 (Draughtsman, Stoker, warden), Pull 2 (runaway, towel-bearer), Swap 2 (notary, tuner), Hop 2 (Copyist, exile). **Starter pages use only Seam runes** (the exile alone knows Board runes, since she arrives only after you hold them), so a recruit is useful in the room where she joins. A school habit (Tally, Hold, Unison) is dormant until the household owns the rune. *Proposal:* the Jar page is learnable before the canals' second landing, so the runaway's habit works at once; otherwise her card shows it dormant like the others.

### How they join

- **Loot offers** (runaway, notary, towel-bearer, warden, tuner): "someone joins you", a fixed authored person at a landmark room in their district, offered again on later trips until taken (§0b). Taking it costs that pick. The runaway is the first, at the canals' second landing ([city-and-districts.md](city-and-districts.md) §5b). The others are met after the canals, in any order; no fixed order is chosen.
- **Door arrival A, the Copyist:** knocks after you have **witnessed your first idiom, once the household already holds the runaway** (so the Seam stays a duo until the canals and the third member is always the canals', §0b). A note under the attic door, then a tap to welcome her.
- **Door arrival B, the exile:** knocks once you **hold all three Board pages and have a proven invention** (*Proposal*; a Glyph at its top tier is an alternative, tune by playing). It follows the hall, not the ending, so it is the household's last arrival until the ending.
- **A tenth** (an arrival after the Seam becomes yours, BL-126) is **parked**, not designed.

### Reading the roster as a trio

Not exhaustive, but each pair has a story:
- **Runaway + Stoker:** pull a foe onto the fire; the Stoker's Fork lights the neighbours.
- **Notary + Draughtsman:** he inks, she tallies; her Swap puts a marked foe where his next cast wants it.
- **Towel-bearer + any burster:** she lays and stands in the cloud, a Spark from someone else storms it.
- **Warden + Tuner:** Hold a foe, then a Unison across two tiles; both are slow-turning pieces.
- **Copyist:** touches from two tiles, a safer close-range caster for the frailest bodies.
- **Exile:** Draw and Erase let her undo or move the setups the others make.

## 3. What stays open

- **The tenth arrival after the ending** (BL-126) and whether the household changes after the Seam becomes yours.
- **Engine hooks** for the new habits and the three new actions are specified in the build plan (BL-43), not here.
